# Voice

The editorial rules **shared by both systems**. These govern every word Start9
publishes, in either register. **When in doubt, re-read this file** — it
outranks any visual preference.

Voice that differs by register — typographic casing, how much technical depth
to expose, what counts as proof — lives in each system's own README:
[professional](systems/professional/README.md#voice-in-this-register),
[consumer](systems/consumer/README.md#voice-in-this-register).

## The core posture

**Tell, don't sell.** Answer three questions plainly and then stop: _What is it?
Why is it interesting? How do you use it?_ No slogans, no persuasion theater, no
"Get Started today."

**Quiet confidence. Restraint is the flex.** We have nothing to prove. The
posture is someone who doesn't need the sale.

**"It's here if you want it; free unless you want to pay."** State the free/paid
split as a fact. Never pressure.

## Rules

### No coercive CTA

"Get Started" is banished. There is no floating global call-to-action anywhere.
Actions are concrete and appear in context:

- **Download** — free software
- **Shop** — hardware
- **Talk to us** — organizations

### Transparent value, not upsell

When something has a price, show the real number and let the buyer do the math.
The honest comparison persuades on its own. Never assert a savings percentage;
show the arithmetic and let the reader conclude.

### Don't dilute the substance

The technology is serious, novel machinery, and the copy says so rather than
smoothing it into benefit-speak. Technical density is balanced — not watered
down — by counterweights interleaved with the hard content: plain-language
descriptions, recognizable open-source names, demonstrations of how easy it
actually is, and a person explaining why. The contrast is the engine.

_How much depth to put in front of a reader is register-specific._ Both systems
refuse to dilute; they disagree about who is expected to keep up. See each
system's README.

### Describe products; put the ideology where it's sought

Product pages describe what the things are. The mission — sovereign computing,
digital autonomy, privacy, dignity — lives on the About page. Available, never
imposed.

### We don't sell; our users tell

Reviews carry the social proof, in customers' own words.

### Never fabricate proof

Where case studies, reviews, or metrics don't exist yet, the page says so
plainly. An honest empty state beats invented social proof.

Never invent a customer name, a case study, a figure, or a contact address. Any
number that appears in a comparison carries a footnote naming its source and
date, so the claim is checkable.

### Equal weight is not equal word count

Prominence and placement carry weight. A crisp claim is purer "tell" than three
paragraphs of depth.

## Register: Professional vs Consumer

Same posture, different volume.

|                | Professional                                              | Consumer                                    |
| -------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Reader         | An organization deciding where its data lives             | An individual who already cares             |
| Emotional key  | Institutional confidence                                  | Enthusiasm, identity                        |
| Proof          | Cost comparisons, warranty terms, support SLAs, ownership | Reviews, the services wall, the mission     |
| Depth          | Legible to a non-technical evaluator                      | Free to go over the reader's head           |
| Casing         | Sentence case everywhere; no tracking                     | Uppercase nav/labels, Title Case headings   |
| Typography     | One text face, no display type                            | Display type carries the headline           |
| Motion & depth | None. Hairlines and tints                                 | Gradients, 3D shadows, glass                |

The professional register is not "the consumer brand, muted." It is built from
the ground up to be legible to someone evaluating a vendor: shorter paragraphs,
concrete nouns, real numbers, no metaphor.
